### ID: 39
**Q:** A patient presents with symptoms of metabolic acidosis. Which of the following compensatory mechanisms would the body primarily employ to restore acid-base balance?
-           0: Increased renal reabsorption of bicarbonate ions.
-           1: Decreased production of ammonia by renal tubules.
- [CORRECT] 2: Increased secretion of hydrogen ions by the kidneys.
-           3: Decreased respiratory rate to retain CO2.

**Exp:** In metabolic acidosis, the body attempts to excrete excess acid and retain bicarbonate. The kidneys respond by increasing the secretion of hydrogen ions (H+) into the urine and increasing the reabsorption of bicarbonate ions. Additionally, the kidneys increase ammonia production to buffer H+ ions. A decreased respiratory rate would worsen acidosis by retaining CO2.

### ID: 40
**Q:** Which of the following hormones primarily promotes the synthesis of bone matrix proteins and the deposition of calcium in bone?
-           0: Parathyroid hormone (PTH).
-           1: Cortisol.
- [CORRECT] 2: Calcitonin.
-           3: Calcitriol (active Vitamin D).

**Exp:** Calcitonin, secreted by the thyroid gland, acts to lower blood calcium levels by inhibiting osteoclast activity and promoting the deposition of calcium into bone, including the synthesis of bone matrix proteins. PTH and calcitriol generally act to raise blood calcium, while cortisol has catabolic effects on bone at high concentrations.

### ID: 111
**Q:** A researcher is studying metabolic pathways in human skeletal muscle during intense, anaerobic sprinting. Which of the following allosteric effectors is the most potent activator of phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1), the rate-limiting step of glycolysis, under these conditions?
-           0: Glucose-6-phosphate
-           1: ATP
-           2: Citrate
- [CORRECT] 3: Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate

**Exp:** Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate is the most potent allosteric activator of PFK-1, overriding the inhibitory effects of high ATP and citrate levels to accelerate glycolysis during energy demand. While high ATP and citrate inhibit PFK-1, Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate powerfully stimulates it. Glucose-6-phosphate inhibits hexokinase, not PFK-1.

### ID: 133
**Q:** Which of the following amino acids serves as an essential nitrogen donor in the biosynthesis of purine nucleotides and is classified as strictly ketogenic?
-           0: Glutamine
- [CORRECT] 1: Leucine
-           2: Alanine
-           3: Aspartate

**Exp:** Leucine and lysine are the only two strictly ketogenic amino acids in humans, meaning their carbon skeletons are degraded exclusively to acetyl-CoA or acetoacetate. Glutamine and aspartate are glucogenic and participate in purine synthesis, but leucine is strictly ketogenic and does not contribute to net gluconeogenesis.

### ID: 143
**Q:** Which of the following amino acids serves as the essential nitrogen donor for the biosynthesis of purines, pyrimidines, and urea, and is classified as a purely ketogenic and glycogenic amino acid?
- [CORRECT] 0: Glutamate
-           1: Alanine
-           2: Lysine
-           3: Leucine

**Exp:** Glutamate acts as a central hub in nitrogen metabolism, donating amino groups for nucleotide and urea synthesis, and can be converted via alpha-ketoglutarate into glucose intermediates while also yielding TCA cycle precursors. Leucine and lysine are purely ketogenic. Alanine is strictly glycogenic.

### ID: 190
**Q:** A researcher is studying the structural stability of various proteins found in the human periodontium. Which of the following amino acids is primarily responsible for forming covalent disulfide bonds that stabilize the tertiary and quaternary structure of extracellular proteins like amelogenin?
-           0: Serine
- [CORRECT] 1: Cysteine
-           2: Glycine
-           3: Methionine

**Exp:** Cysteine contains a thiol (-SH) group that can oxidize to form covalent disulfide bonds (-S-S-) with another cysteine residue, stabilizing protein tertiary and quaternary structures. Methionine also contains sulfur but does not form disulfide bonds. Serine can undergo phosphorylation, and glycine provides flexibility due to its small side chain.

### ID: 371
**Q:** A 4-year-old boy presents with developmental regression, cherry-red spots on the macula, and progressive hepatosplenomegaly, but without bone lesions. Enzyme assay reveals a deficiency in lysosomal beta-glucocerebrosidase. This clinical and biochemical profile is diagnostic of which lysosomal storage disorder?
- [CORRECT] 0: Gaucher disease
-           1: Niemann-Pick disease
-           2: Tay-Sachs disease
-           3: Hurler syndrome

**Exp:** Gaucher disease is caused by a deficiency of beta-glucocerebrosidase, leading to the accumulation of glucosylceramide (glucocerebroside) in macrophages, manifesting as hepatosplenomegaly, bone crises, and sometimes CNS involvement (Type 2/3). Note: While classic Tay-Sachs features a cherry-red spot and no hepatosplenomegaly (due to hexosaminidase A deficiency), Gaucher characteristically presents with hepatosplenomegaly and Gaucher cells; Niemann-Pick features sphingomyelinase deficiency with cherry-red spots and hepatosplenomegaly.

### ID: 439
**Q:** During a rigorous 40-minute dental procedure, a nervous dental assistant experiences hyperventilation and mild respiratory alkalosis. Which of the following allosteric effectors of hemoglobin will rapidly compensate to increase oxygen unloading to the tissues?
-           0: Decreased carbon dioxide tension
- [CORRECT] 1: Decreased pH (Bohr effect)
-           2: Increased ATP binding
-           3: Increased 2,3-Bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-BPG)

**Exp:** In acute respiratory alkalosis, blood pH rises, which initially shifts the oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve to the left (increasing affinity). However, local tissue metabolic demands and rapid allosteric adjustments involve the Bohr effect, where decreased pH and increased CO2 promote oxygen release. 2,3-BPG takes hours to days to adapt significantly, making it incorrect for an acute 40-minute event. Decreased CO2 would actually increase affinity, not help unload oxygen.

### ID: 448
**Q:** A 28-year-old female presents with fatigue, microcytic anemia, and glossitis. Further biochemical evaluation demonstrates an inherited defect in the cytosolic conversion of homocysteine to methionine. Which essential cofactor is most likely deficient or involved in this pathway?
-           0: Ascorbic acid (Vitamin C)
- [CORRECT] 1: Cobalamin (Vitamin B12)
-           2: Thiamine pyrophosphate (Vitamin B1)
-           3: Pyridoxal phosphate (Vitamin B6)

**Exp:** Methionine synthase requires vitamin B12 (cobalamin) as a cofactor to transfer a methyl group from N5-methyltetrahydrofolate to homocysteine, forming methionine. Vitamin B6 is involved in transamination and cystathionine beta-synthase steps, while B1 and C are involved in different metabolic pathways.

### ID: 750790537
**Q:** A 28-year-old pregnant female patient in her second trimester presents with a localized, rapidly growing, pedunculated lesion on the interdental papilla between her maxillary right central and lateral incisors. The lesion is soft, red, bleeds easily upon probing, and is asymptomatic. She reports it first appeared about a month ago. What is the MOST appropriate initial management?
-           0: Immediate surgical excision with wide margins.
-           1: Prescribe topical corticosteroids to reduce inflammation.
- [CORRECT] 2: Reassure the patient, provide oral hygiene instructions, and monitor.
-           3: Provide thorough oral hygiene instructions, consider conservative excision if persistent or symptomatic, preferably post-partum.

**Exp:** The description is classic for a pyogenic granuloma (pregnancy tumor). Initial management should focus on improving oral hygiene, as these lesions are often exacerbated by local irritants. While some may regress postpartum, excision is often necessary if the lesion persists, interferes with function, or causes aesthetic concerns. This is typically done conservatively and often deferred until after delivery if possible, unless severely problematic. Immediate wide excision is overly aggressive, and corticosteroids are ineffective for this type of lesion.
